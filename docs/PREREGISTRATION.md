## 1. Title

Neuro-Cognitive Information Design: a within-subjects eye-tracking study of
document readability across 12 professions.

## 2. Authors

Ardit Ceno (contact: arditceno82@gmail.com).

## 3. Research questions

1. Does an optimised layout improve comprehension accuracy?
2. Does it reduce reading time and perceived workload?
3. Does it change fixation duration, regressions and time to first fixation?

## 4. Hypotheses

- H1: dense formats → longer fixations and more regressions.
- H2: optimised formats → shorter TTFF and fewer errors.
- H3: structured formats → higher comprehension.

## 5. Design

Within-subjects, counterbalanced A/B; 12 professions × 3 expertise levels ×
3 modules. Order by session parity. One calibration pass (9 points); manual
recalibration allowed.

## 6. Sample

- Population: adults 18+ working in, or training for, one of the 12 professions.
- Target N: **80** after attrition (65 required before attrition; see
  `docs/POWER.md`). Recruitment goal: 120 (10 per profession) for descriptive
  coverage.
- Exclusion: calibration error above threshold, gaze coverage < 60%, session
  shorter than the pilot minimum, consent withdrawn.

## 7. Materials

108 modules; 432 documents (A/B × EN/AL); 648 quiz items; one diagram per module.

## 8. Outcomes

- Primary: comprehension accuracy, reading time, NASA-TLX.
- Secondary: fixation duration, regressions, TTFF, AOI dwell, preference.
- Exploratory: blink rate, head pose.

## 9. Analysis

Mixed-effects models with random intercepts for participant and module; effect
sizes with 95% CI; Holm–Bonferroni for primary outcomes; BH for secondary.
Full plan: `docs/ANALYSIS_PLAN.md`.
