Introduction

Why document format matters

Professionals read dense documents every day, but their visual layout is rarely designed with evidence from reading research.

Two opposing strategies coexist:

  • Structured (A): generous spacing, clear visual hierarchy.
  • Compressed (B): maximum content per page.

Formatting influences where readers look, how fast they find information, and how much mental effort they spend (Rayner, 1998; Sweller, 2011).

Gap: existing guidance is mostly stylistic or based on non-professional texts.

Aim & Objectives

What we measure and compare

Aim: quantify how format affects comprehension, reading effort and mental workload across professions and expertise.

  • Comprehension accuracy (quiz per format)
  • Eye-movement effort (fixations, regressions)
  • Perceived workload (NASA-TLX)
  • Interaction with profession & expertise

Methodology

Counterbalanced A/B, in the browser

Design. Within-subjects, counterbalanced. 12 professions × 3 expertise × 3 modules × 2 formats.

Materials. 108 documents (A + B versions of the same content).

Platform. WebGazer + MediaPipe FaceMesh; calibration with bias correction.

Results · direction

What we expect to find

  • Format A (structured) supports comprehension and reduces re-reading, especially for lower-expertise readers.
  • Experts show smaller format effects (domain schemas compensate for density).
  • Compressed format (B) increases scanning effort (regressions, fixation duration).
  • Workload (NASA-TLX) is higher for B in information-heavy modules.

Discussion

Format is a cognitive design decision

If A improves accuracy and reduces re-reading for non-experts, professional and educational documents should prioritise hierarchy and spacing over density.

Format effects that differ by profession show that no single universal rule suffices.

Practical implication: a checklist for professional documents — clear headings, lists, whitespace, controlled line length.
