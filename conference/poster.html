Neuro-Cognitive Information Design: Structured vs. Compressed Document Formats Across 12 Professions

Ardit Ceno
[Institution, Faculty, City] · arditceno1@gmail.com

Introduction

Professionals read dense texts daily, yet document layout is rarely designed with evidence from reading research. Two opposing strategies coexist: a structured format (generous spacing, clear hierarchy) and a compressed format (maximum content per page). Formatting affects where readers look, how easily they find information, and how much mental effort they spend (Rayner, 1998; Sweller, 2011). Existing guidance is mostly stylistic; this study measures the effect of format on comprehension and eye movement across 12 professional domains.

Objectives

  • Compare comprehension between format A and format B (separate quiz per format).
  • Compare eye-movement effort: fixation duration, saccadic regressions, time to first fixation, reading time.
  • Compare perceived workload (NASA-TLX) per module.
  • Test whether the best format depends on profession and expertise.

Methodology

Design. Within-subjects, counterbalanced A/B. 12 professions × 3 expertise levels × 3 modules × 2 formats.

Materials. 108 documents (structured and compressed versions of the same content).

Measures. Comprehension accuracy; gaze (fixation, regressions, TTFF, reading time); NASA-TLX; blink rate; head pose (yaw/pitch/roll).

Platform. Browser-based eye-tracking (WebGazer + MediaPipe FaceMesh), calibration with bias correction.

Discussion

Format is a design decision with measurable cognitive consequences. If the structured format improves accuracy and reduces re-reading for non-experts, professional and educational documents should prioritise visual hierarchy and spacing over density. Differences by profession would indicate that a single universal rule is insufficient and that design must be domain-aware.

Conclusion

This work provides evidence-based, cross-domain guidance for document design, and a reproducible web-based eye-tracking instrument. [Final conclusion to be added after data collection.]
